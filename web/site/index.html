ZEN-1 禅

Ambient firmware for the M-VAVE FM-1 that turns it into a small living window. A cherry tree drops petals on a river, koi circle a pond, fireflies blink over a summer stream under a real moon, and every movement you see is a note you hear.

Switch it on, set it down and let it play: soft, slow and never quite the same. It is made for listening while you read, work, wind down or fall asleep. When you want to, reach in: the keys play along with the scene, and the knobs shape every part of it.

Version __VERSION__.

  • Installing is at your own risk: read the notes on the install page first.

Just listen, or make it yours

Made for listening

There is nothing to do. Pick a scene and it plays on its own for as long as you like, its music unhurried and gentle, the light slowly turning from morning to night. A soft drone breathes underneath, notes come and go like rain, and every few minutes the music pauses for a breath. It is calm on purpose: no beats, no sudden sounds, no flashing lights, nothing that jumps.

Yours to tweak

Every scene has the same four pages of knobs:
  • Scene: the mood, how busy, the instrument, the room
  • Volume: the notes, the drone, the air, the water
  • Nature: which sounds those are, and the scene's own touches
  • Music: echo, warmth, phrases and the tide
Pick a mood to set them all at once, then adjust anything you like. Every mood remembers your tweaks, so your Morning stays your Morning.

Three scenes

Sakura 桜

A pale cherry leans over a narrow city stream, its far bank lined with more cherries, all mirrored in the water.
  • Each petal that lands plays a koto note, pitched by where it lands.
  • Gusts shake loose a shower of petals with a harp run.
  • A bush warbler sings; spring showers come and go.
  • At night the tree is lit and paper lanterns glow along the far bank.

Koi 鯉

Koi of the real varieties glide, hover and follow each other in a garden pond, a new shape with each picture.
  • Every bite of food is a note: low in the deep middle, high near the edge.
  • Play a key and they come over to you.
  • Japanese pond frogs hop onto the lily pads, and a bamboo shishi-odoshi knocks.
  • Rain rings the water; at night a stone lantern lights the pond.

Hotaru 蛍

Fireflies drift over a summer stream, each blinking on its own clock, until the whole field falls into one shared pulse, as real fireflies do.
  • Each blink is a soft note.
  • The moon, from NASA's lunar map, takes the path its phase really takes.
  • Frogs call and an owl hoots.

What makes it deep

Music that comes from the world

  • Nothing is a loop. Every note comes from something happening on screen: a petal landing, a koi biting, a firefly blinking, a pebble dropping. Where, how fast and how near decide which note and how loud.
  • A breathing drone of three voices, each swelling and fading on its own cycle, so the bed under the notes is always shifting and never stops.
  • Tide: a slow ebb and flow, three to six minutes end to end, from sparse, dark and echoing to full and bright. Every few minutes the notes rest for a breath, and the world goes on without them.
  • Phrases: notes can come from four loops of unequal length that slide past each other, so melodies recur but never line up the same way twice.
  • Japanese scales, chosen so every key you play sounds right over the music:
    • Yo (bright) by day
    • Min'yo (warm) from golden hour through the night
    • In (dark) only for Hotaru's Deep Night
    • Natural follows the light, and now and then at dawn lets the key drift a fourth or fifth.
  • Sixteen keys, sixteen notes: every white key is its own note, rising left to right, and all of them glow softly. Any key sounds right: notes outside the chord being played come as gentle passing notes.
  • Real FM synthesis (Dexed's DX7-class engine): koto, harp, bells, a glass furin, water drops and mallets, with echoes, reverb and a Warmth control that mellows the whole sound.
  • Sounds are heard where they happen: a raindrop landing on the left of the pond is heard on the left, and a firefly high in the sky sounds higher.

Moods and time

  • Natural runs a whole day or night in half an hour, and the music follows the light: livelier by day, quieter and warmer at night.
  • Four moods per scene, each with its own time and character:
    • Sakura: Morning, Afternoon, Golden Hour, Night
    • Koi: Feeding, Rain, Still Pond, Night
    • Hotaru: Dusk, Festival, Quiet Night, Deep Night
  • Nothing jumps. Changing mood glides the scene there over thirty seconds: the light turns, lanterns light one at a time, the moon slides across the sky while its shadow sweeps to the new phase.
  • Every mood remembers your tweaks:
    • Adjust a mood and it stays yours, marked with a small dot, even after a power-off.
    • Switch moods and come back, and it is as you left it.
    • A tweaked Natural still runs its whole day.
    • Turn just past a tweaked mood onto "Reset" and it goes back to how it was made.

Small things worth noticing

  • Six real Japanese frogs visit the koi pond, each in its own colors and markings, each as common as it would be at a garden pond, and each croaking at a pitch to match its size. The koi turn to look when one plops in.
  • A real moon: Hotaru's moon is drawn from NASA's Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter map, with its true seas.
    • Full, gibbous, half or crescent.
    • A full moon rises at dusk and is highest at midnight; a crescent sets early.
    • A full moon makes the music brighter; a crescent makes it darker, with longer echoes.
  • Fireflies that really synchronize: each nudges its neighbors toward its own timing, so the field drifts into one gentle pulse, holds it, and drifts apart again.
  • Stars as they are: scattered in loose clusters, a few faintly blue or warm, the brightest first to come out, dimmed near the moon and low in the haze. Shooting stars pass with a chime, and on a Deep Night the Milky Way appears.
  • Weather:
    • Mist rolls in over Hotaru's field in soft fog banks.
    • Rain on Hotaru rings the stream and splashes on the reeds, and the fireflies shelter on the reeds, glowing slowly.
    • Rain on the koi pond is seen and heard drop by drop.
    • Rain begins with a few drops and builds, and when it stops the last drops still fall.
  • Recorded nature, all public domain: a brook, a breeze in the trees, crickets, a bush warbler's song, a frog, an owl and a bamboo knock, mixed with synthesized wind, water and rain.
  • The pond remembers: food a full koi leaves can sink and come back later as a soft bowl note.
  • Painterly or pixel art, at the press of a button, for every scene.
  • Calm by design:
    • The panel's lights glow softly, and only where a button does something.
    • The screen shows only the scene until you touch something.
    • A tap on HOME shows the battery, the scene and the mood for a moment.

Play along

  • White keys play the scene's instrument, and the scene answers:
    • Sakura: the blossoms shake, and two petals land later with your note.
    • Koi: the koi find the food you dropped and play your melody back in their own order.
    • Hotaru: a firefly rises to your note's height and joins the rhythm.
  • Black keys are each scene's own:
    • Sakura: a harp whose notes ring out anywhere on the river.
    • Koi: pebbles on singing bowls, and the shishi-odoshi's knock.
    • Hotaru: firefly flashes, and a breeze that carries the fireflies away.
  • FuMi-1's koto instrument is still there as a mode: hold HOME.
